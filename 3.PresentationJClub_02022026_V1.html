Introduction

Introduction

(Flournoy 1893)

Space-Sequence Synesthesia is a perceptual condition in which ordinal sequences (e.g., numbers, days of the week, or months of the year) are experienced as occupying specific spatial locations (M. C. Price 2009; Rothen et al. 2016; Ward et al. 2018)

SSS: people experience a particular spatial order for sequences.

Inducer : a sequence

concurrent: spatial place

Who is a genuine synesthete?

Suggested criteria (Seron et al. 1992; Deroy and Spence 2013)

  1. Conscious

  2. Developmentally early

  3. Automatic

  4. Unidirectional

  1. Consistent in time

Consistency tests

Task: “Click on the spatial location elicited by the present stimulus”

  • Measure of spatial position’s variability over time (Baron-Cohen et al. 1993)

  • Triangle area (Rothen et al. 2016)

  • Limits (i..e Ward et al. 2018) :

    • High consistency if same or close coordinates

    • Might favour linear over circular forms

    • Ordinality between items is ignored

Tested for weekdays, months and digits (0 to 9). 3 repetitions each.

Present study

Optimizing diagnostics from consistency tests

  • stage I: Find a method that best classifies Space-Sequence Synaesthesia and Controls with pre-existing data.
  • stage II: Make predictions of optimal method for to-be-collected data (Sachdeva et al., 2024, Stage 1 in-principle acceptance)

Methods

  • laboratory settings
    • Rothen et al. (2016)
    • Van Petersen et al. (2020)
  • Online testing
    • Ward (2022)

  • Coordinates individually standardized (z-scores)

  • n = 2 excluded because same x or y coordinates across conditions and repetitions. n = 2 excluded for having less than 4 (x,y) points

  • 0.02 % empty trials excluded (i.e. skipped responses)

Feature extraction

Area

Perimeter

\[ Perimeter = \sqrt{(x2 - x1)^2 + (y2 – y1)^2} + \sqrt{(x3 - x2)^2 + (y3 –y2)^2} + \sqrt{(x1 - x3)^2 + (y1 – y3)^2} \tag{2}\]

Self-intersections

Figure 4: Example slef-intersections

First, we extract the number of self-intersections of each segments. Conceptually, SSS should have less chance to produce that self-intersect than control. The number of self-intersections are added separately for each repetitions and conditions and averaged per participants.

Topological validity (“PostGIS 3.6.2dev Manual,” n.d.)

sf simple features package (Pebesma 2018):

  1. Polygon rings are simple (i.e., they do not touch or self-intersect)

  2. Boundary rings to not cross

  3. Boundary rings may only touch points tangentially

  4. Rings that define holes are contained within the exterior ring

  5. Polygon rings must not splits the polygon

We assessed topological validity using geometric validation test.

Topological validity tests if a polygons is well-formed and valid according to the Open Geospatial Consortium (OGC) Simple Features Specification (Herring 2010).

Results

However, at the proposed cut-off, the permuted validity leads to higher specificity (78.55 vs. 75.78) hence best to reject controls (i.e. less false negatives) but smaller sensitivity (70.781 vs. 72.544), hence perimeter is best at detecting SSS (i.e. leads to less false positives). See Figure 9 in ?@sec-sm1-feature-distributions for the densities of each standardized features suggesting bi-modal distribution (see also difference in median, see ?@tbl-mytb05.

In sum, the permuted validity and perimeter both provide similarly best AUC compared to the other features. While the permuted validity is best to have less false negatives (i.e. controls) while perimeter is best for less false positives (i.e. SSS).
